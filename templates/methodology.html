Methodology

This page exists so that anyone can check us, reproduce us, or disagree with a specific knob rather than with the whole clock. Three kinds of numbers appear on the site: official figures shown as they are, one estimate built from them with a four-factor formula whose every factor is a slider, and measurements from real systems.

The estimate: four factors

burn per year = base spend × inference share × agent share × avoidable share
burn per second = burn per year / 31,557,600

Multiply the defaults and the world scope gives about 6.3 % of AI spend, roughly $163 billion a year, burning on reading that an index would have avoided. Move any slider and the page recomputes. We would rather you argue with a factor than with the clock.

What "retrieval-first" means

An agent that needs one function today typically searches by reading: it greps the tree, opens files, and pays for every byte it loads into its context window. Retrieval-first means a local index answers "where is it" first and the agent reads only the passage. The software that does this is open source; XERJ is one implementation and the one we measured. The clock is not a product page: any index that returns passages instead of files produces the same effect.

What the clock does not claim

It does not claim that AI causes public debt; the debt figure is context and scale. It does not claim the federal figure is total federal AI spending; it is the labelled floor. It does not claim the world figure is precise; it is a Gartner forecast multiplied by three stated shares. Everything is reproducible from /data.json and the open pipeline.

Corrections

If a number is wrong, write to hello@aiburnclock.org or open an issue. Corrections are published on this page with the date.
